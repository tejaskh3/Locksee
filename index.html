InsomniacDev

Keep building while you grab a coffee — or even while you sleep.

Download for macOS

51 KB · macOS 13 or newer · Apple silicon & Intel

What it is

One Swift file, no dependencies, living in your menu bar. It does two things. It drops a shield over every display and swallows your keyboard, mouse and trackpad until you Touch ID back in — and it holds your Mac awake with real power assertions, so a long job doesn't die just because the machine decided to turn in for the night.

Worth being straight about: the lock is a don't touch my machine guard for when you step away from your desk, not a security boundary. It's a window and an event tap, so anyone who can reach the machine over SSH can end it. For a real boundary, use ⌃⌘Q.

Setting it up

  1. Unzip and drag InsomniacDev to your Applications folder.
  2. First launch: right-click the app and choose Open. It's signed with a self-signed certificate rather than a paid Apple developer ID, so double-clicking it the first time gets you a Gatekeeper warning. Right-click ▸ Open gives you the button to proceed. Once only.
  3. Grant Accessibility when asked — System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Accessibility. That's what lets it block input while locked; without it you'd get a shield that stops nothing.

How to use

  1. Lock — press ⌃⌘L, double-click the menu bar icon, or pick Lock Screen from the menu.
  2. Unlock — touch the Touch ID sensor. The shield stays up and input stays blocked for the whole prompt.
  3. Keep awake — click the icon and hit Keep Awake, or use Start For to pick a span. The icon turns into an eye while a session is running.
  4. Set the guardrails — a battery cutoff so it switches itself off when you get low, and whether the display stays lit or goes dark.

What's in the menu

  • Lock Shortcut⌃⌘L, ⌥⌘L, ⌃⇧L or ⌃⌥⌘L — or off entirely.
  • Block Trackpad GesturesSwallow scroll, pinch, rotate and swipe while locked, not just clicks.
  • Start For5 minutes to 5 hours, or no time limit, with the countdown in the menu.
  • Low Battery CutoffEnds the session at 10–40%, so it can't flatten your battery unattended.
  • Also Keep Display OnOff by default — work keeps running with the screen dark.
  • Launch at LoginStart with the Mac, and optionally start caffeinated too.

Known limits

Nothing keeps a Mac awake through a closed lid — macOS forces sleep on clamshell regardless. Sleep you ask for explicitly isn't blocked either; only idle sleep is. And a display plugged in while the screen is already locked won't be covered until you unlock and lock again.
