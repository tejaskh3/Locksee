What it is

One Swift file, no dependencies, living in your menu bar. It does two things. It drops a shield over every display and swallows your keyboard, mouse and trackpad until you Touch ID back in — and it holds your Mac awake with real power assertions, so a long job doesn't die just because the machine decided to turn in for the night.

Worth being straight about: the lock is a don't touch my machine guard for when you step away from your desk, not a security boundary. It's a window and an event tap, so anyone who can reach the machine over SSH can end it. For a real boundary, use ⌃⌘Q.

Setting it up

  1. Unzip and drag InsomniacDev to your Applications folder.
  2. First launch: right-click the app and choose Open. It's signed with a self-signed certificate rather than a paid Apple developer ID, so double-clicking it the first time gets you a Gatekeeper warning. Right-click ▸ Open gives you the button to proceed. Once only.
  3. Grant Accessibility when asked — System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Accessibility. That's what lets it block input while locked; without it you'd get a shield that stops nothing.

How to use

  1. Lock — press ⌃⌘L, double-click the menu bar icon, or pick Lock Screen from the menu.
  2. Unlock — touch the Touch ID sensor. The shield stays up and input stays blocked for the whole prompt.
  3. Keep awake — click the icon and hit Keep Awake, or use Start For to pick a span. The icon turns into an eye while a session is running.
  4. Set the guardrails — a battery cutoff so it switches itself off when you get low, and whether the display stays lit or goes dark.

What's in the menu

  • Lock Shortcut⌃⌘L, ⌥⌘L, ⌃⇧L or ⌃⌥⌘L — or off entirely.
  • Block Trackpad GesturesSwallow scroll, pinch, rotate and swipe while locked, not just clicks.
  • Start For5 minutes to 5 hours, or no time limit, with the countdown in the menu.
  • Low Battery CutoffEnds the session at 10–40%, so it can't flatten your battery unattended.
  • Also Keep Display OnOff by default — work keeps running with the screen dark.
  • Launch at LoginStart with the Mac, and optionally start caffeinated too.

When it works

SituationThis appcaffeinate
You lock the screen and walk away Works Works

Power assertions belong to the process, not to your session's UI, so locking doesn't touch them. Confirmed on a real run: the keep-awake and screen-locked assertions were held simultaneously by the same process. This is the case the app is built for — lock up, walk off, build keeps going.

You close the lid Sleeps Sleeps

Closing the lid triggers a different sleep path, one that power assertions don't touch. So no assertion-based tool survives it — not this one, not caffeinate, and not KeepingYouAwake, whose own FAQ says it "will only prevent sleep on desktop Macs and portable Macs with an open lid." Getting past a closed lid needs a different mechanism altogether: clamshell mode, a root-level pmset change, or one of the newer tools that read the lid-angle sensor on recent Apple silicon. Worth remembering why macOS does this — a laptop that never sleeps in a closed bag gets hot.

Lid closed, on power, external display + keyboard Works Works

This is macOS's own clamshell mode rather than anything an app does — the system simply declines to sleep when those conditions are met. You don't need either tool for it.

You choose Sleep from the Apple menu Sleeps Sleeps

Only idle sleep is blocked. Sleep you ask for explicitly is honoured — which is what you'd want, since the alternative is a Mac that ignores you.

Battery drops to 15% while unattended Stops itself Runs it flat

The one real difference in day-to-day use. Set a cutoff between 10% and 40% and the session ends itself when you hit it. caffeinate has no battery awareness: left running on battery, it will happily hold your Mac awake until it dies.

You want input blocked while you're away from the desk Works n/a

The shield swallows keys, clicks and trackpad gestures until you Touch ID back in. caffeinate doesn't do this — it isn't a locker. One caveat: displays are shielded as they were when you locked, so a monitor plugged in mid-lock isn't covered until you unlock and lock again.
